The experimental evaluation is comprehensive and rigorous, demonstrating consistent improvements across four diverse domains and showing that small models can significantly outperform much larger ones. Particularly noteworthy is the validation in Section 3.3 correlating posterior approximation quality with downstream performance, which provides strong evidence that the probabilistic perspective is not merely conceptually clean but empirically meaningful. The approach achieves notable computational efficiency gains from incrementality (5-10x fewer particles than non-incremental methods). Minor weaknesses include limited guidance on when the approach provides the most benefit, incomplete characterization of computational overhead relative to alternatives, and lack of theoretical analysis of sample complexity. The requirement for domain-specific grammars and potentials, while presented as a feature, may limit adoption. Nevertheless, these weaknesses are not fundamental and do not undermine the core contributions. The paper advances the field meaningfully by demonstrating how principled probabilistic inference can improve constrained generation, with practical benefits validated across diverse tasks.

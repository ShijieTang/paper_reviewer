The core idea of In-Run Data Shapley is conceptually sound and addresses real limitations of retraining-based approaches. The 'ghost' techniques for efficient gradient computations are technically elegant and achieve impressive efficiency improvements (~30x over naive methods, minimal overhead for first-order). The empirical findings from Pile pretraining are interesting and potentially impactful for data curation and copyright discussions. However, the work has several notable limitations that prevent it from being a strong accept: (1) The validation data requirement is a fundamental constraint not fully addressed. (2) The experimental scope is limited—only GPT2 and Pythia-410M are tested, which are far smaller than actual foundation models, so claims about foundation model scalability are largely unverified. (3) The Adam optimizer limitation is significant given its dominance in modern deep learning. (4) Second-order variants have 2x overhead, limiting practical adoption. (5) Approximation accuracy is only validated at one iteration with fixed hyperparameters. Despite these limitations, the work is technically sound, introduces genuine novelty (both conceptual and technical), and demonstrates practical utility. It would be a reasonable accept for a top-tier venue, particularly given the timeliness of foundation model data attribution research and the novel insights from empirical analysis, but it is not exceptional due to the experimental scope limitations and unresolved practical constraints."

      "summary_comment": "This paper makes a solid contribution to mechanistic interpretability by demonstrating that LLMs possess linear representations of political ideology along the American liberal-conservative axis. The work is technically sound, well-executed, and novel in applying probing and steering techniques to political perspective. The experimental validation spans multiple validation levels including cross-domain generalization (lawmakers to news outlets) and causal steering with human evaluation. Key strengths include rigorous methodology, practical demonstrations of steering, and evidence of genuine ideological representation rather than memorization. However, significant limitations restrict the impact: (1) findings apply only to 7B models, not larger or smaller models, (2) generalization to non-U.S. or less polarized contexts is untested, (3) mechanistic understanding of *why* particular heads capture ideology remains unclear, and (4) the media outlet generalization test, while clever, validates on a correlated measure of the same latent construct rather than truly independent behavioral outcomes. The steering experiment relies heavily on GPT-4o validation on only 1.9% of essays, which is a concern. Despite these limitations, the work advances understanding of how LLMs represent subjective concepts and provides practical tools for monitoring and controlling ideological content. The contribution is meaningful for the interpretability community and relevant to responsible AI deployment, though not groundbreaking. The paper merits publication at a top-tier venue as a solid mechanistic interpretability contribution with interesting societal implications, though the scope limitations and incomplete mechanistic understanding prevent it from being exceptional."

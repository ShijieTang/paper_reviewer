      "summary_comment": "This is a well-executed paper that makes a solid technical contribution to neural image compression. The core idea of replacing Gaussian noise with uniform noise in diffusion models to enable efficient universal quantization is clever and addresses a genuine computational bottleneck in prior work (Theis et al., 2022). The theoretical analysis showing convergence to Gaussian diffusion in the continuous limit is rigorous and provides important validation. Experimentally, UQDM achieves competitive rate-distortion performance with a single progressive model across multiple bitrates, outperforming classical codecs and matching recent neural methods like CTC on ImageNet 64×64. The work demonstrates clear practical advantages for progressive compression. However, there are notable limitations: (1) a persistent performance gap to the theoretical ideal of VDM (~2.2 bpd on toy data), (2) limited concrete evidence of computational tractability benefits with wall-clock times showing 5 minutes per 32×32 image with learned variance, and (3) incomplete exploration of design choices (alternative architectures, single-step training). The paper is honest about these limitations but does not adequately investigate their severity or fundamental nature. Overall, this is a meaningful step toward practical diffusion-based progressive compression that meets the bar for a top-tier ML venue, though it represents an incremental rather than transformative advance. The theoretical contributions are solid, experimental validation is thorough, and the practical benefits are demonstrated, even if not yet revolutionary."

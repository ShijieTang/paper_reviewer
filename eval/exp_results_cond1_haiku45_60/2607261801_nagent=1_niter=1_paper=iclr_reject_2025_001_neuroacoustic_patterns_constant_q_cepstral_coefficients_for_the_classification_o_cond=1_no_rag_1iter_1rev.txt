While the application to speech-based disease detection is relevant, the paper suffers from fundamental weaknesses across all dimensions. The core novelty is severely limited—CQCC was introduced in 2017 and already applied to pathological classification in 2023; this work merely transfers the existing method to a different disease classification task. The experimental methodology is deeply flawed: SMOTE is applied before train-test splitting (causing leakage), there is no cross-validation or statistical significance testing, and datasets are extremely small (< 300 samples per class). Most critically, the results are suspicious and internally inconsistent—Table 4 reports 99% accuracy for RF+CQCC on a small dataset without cross-validation, while other tables show very different accuracies (90.3%, 86.1%) across different splits. The main theoretical claim about form-invariance properties ensuring robustness is never empirically validated. The paper lacks comparisons to state-of-the-art methods, provides missing experimental details that prevent reproduction, and contains numerous presentation issues. The work reads as a straightforward application of an existing feature extraction method to a new dataset rather than a methodological or theoretical contribution. For a top-tier ML conference, this falls well short of the required standard."

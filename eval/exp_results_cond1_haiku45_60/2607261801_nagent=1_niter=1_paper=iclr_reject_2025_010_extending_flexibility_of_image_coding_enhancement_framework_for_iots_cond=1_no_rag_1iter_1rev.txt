      "summary_comment": "This paper proposes Easz, an edge-optimized image compression framework that erases portions of images on the edge device and reconstructs them on the server using a lightweight transformer. While the practical motivation is sound, the paper has fundamental technical and methodological issues that prevent acceptance at a top-tier venue. First, the core contribution—a conditional uniform-based sampler with row-based random sampling constraints—is poorly motivated and not clearly distinguished from existing random downsampling approaches. The constraints (δ and Δ) appear ad-hoc and are never ablated. Second, the comparison with super-resolution methods is misleading: this work solves a different problem (erasing content + predicting it) than super-resolution (reconstructing from degraded but complete images). Third, the experimental evaluation is problematic: (a) the improvements in Table 2 conflate pixel erasure (which reduces file size) with reconstruction quality gains, making it impossible to assess the true contribution; (b) the use of only non-reference perceptual metrics avoids the fundamental question of content fidelity; (c) the evaluation on small benchmarks (Kodak, CLIC) without ablations of key design choices is insufficient. Fourth, critical design parameters are never given concrete values or explored systematically. While efficiency improvements on edge hardware are noteworthy, they come from a combination of techniques (downsampling + transformer reconstruction) rather than a novel core contribution. The paper would benefit from clearer motivation, more rigorous experimental design, fair comparisons using reference metrics, and thorough ablations of design choices."

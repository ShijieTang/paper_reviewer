While the proportionality framework is theoretically interesting and the comprehensive experimental scope is commendable, the work has critical weaknesses that prevent acceptance at a top-tier venue: (1) Limited novelty - the core contribution is an incremental modification (EMA weighting) over Niehaus et al. (2024), and no direct experimental comparison validates claimed advantages. (2) Unsound theory - Theorem 2's central claim depends on an unjustified assumption about constant-bounded gradient updates; the connection between O(√t) growth and per-step boundedness is not established. (3) Weak experimental results - SWR doesn't consistently outperform L2 regularization, sometimes significantly underperforms (Table 5), and requires architectural tweaks (separate λf, λc) and ad-hoc fixes (SWR + re-init) that contradict the simplicity claims. (4) Incomplete evaluation - missing comparisons with recent plasticity methods, no ablations on key design choices, limited statistical validation (3 seeds for VGG-16), and unclear hyperparameter selection criteria. The balancedness argument, while intuitive, lacks rigorous empirical validation.

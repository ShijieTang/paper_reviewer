      "summary_comment": "This paper makes a solid theoretical contribution by applying quotient manifold Riemannian geometry to address parameter space symmetries in transformers, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions. The core insight—that GL(h) symmetries in attention mechanisms induce geometric curvature requiring quotient manifold treatment—is novel and well-executed mathematically. Empirically, the work demonstrates meaningful recovery of correlations between sharpness and generalization on transformers where adaptive sharpness fails, a concrete improvement. However, the work has notable limitations: (1) the sign of correlations varies inconsistently across tasks without clear mechanistic explanation, limiting practical use for regularization; (2) experiments span only 2 datasets with model families from specific sources; (3) the full-rank assumption relies on ad-hoc regularization lacking principled justification; (4) presentation complexity may limit accessibility. The paper makes sufficient theoretical and empirical progress to merit publication at a top venue, establishing a principled framework for handling symmetries and recovering previously lost correlations. However, the unresolved inconsistencies and limited experimental scope prevent this from being a definitive solution to generalization prediction for transformers."

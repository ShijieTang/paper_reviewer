The key theoretical insight—that optimal low-rank OT factors co-cluster points with their Monge map images (Proposition 3.1)—is elegant and well-proved, providing a principled foundation for the hierarchical approach. The experimental validation across synthetic benchmarks, transcriptomics, and imaging data is convincing, with HiRef consistently achieving competitive or superior primal costs compared to full-rank (Sinkhorn, ProgOT) and low-rank baselines. However, the work has notable limitations: (1) the approach relies on strict r-Monge separability, a strong geometric condition with unclear practical implications and verification; (2) there is a gap between theoretical optimality guarantees (assuming optimal LROT) and practical implementation (using heuristic solvers); (3) the requirement for equal-sized datasets is restrictive; (4) experimental analysis could be deeper (e.g., when does strict separability hold? why exactly does Sinkhorn fail?). Despite these limitations, the paper represents a meaningful advance for large-scale optimal transport, directly addressing a widely-recognized bottleneck in the field, and the theoretical contributions are non-trivial. The work is suitable for acceptance at a top-tier venue, though not in the very top tier due to the theory-practice gap and restricted applicability."

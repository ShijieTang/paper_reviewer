      "summary_comment": "This is a well-executed paper that makes solid theoretical and practical contributions to machine learning-powered iterative combinatorial auctions. The key insight—that demand and value queries have complementary strengths and should be combined in a specific order with careful handling (bridge bid)—is both novel and well-validated. The theoretical framework (Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.2) rigorously establishes this insight, and the mixed training algorithm properly exploits both query types. Experimentally, MLHCA demonstrates substantial improvements over prior work across all tested domains, with practical significance for billion-dollar spectrum auctions. The paper thoughtfully addresses implementation details including activity rules, payment mechanisms, and incentive considerations. However, several weaknesses prevent this from being an exceptional contribution: (1) evaluation limited to synthetic domains without real auction data, (2) incomplete incentive guarantees relying on strong assumptions, (3) MLHCA's VQ rounds received domain-specific HPO while baselines used fixed parameters, potentially inflating performance differences, and (4) missing ablations on key design choices like the 40/60 DQ/VQ split. These issues are fixable (mostly through additional experiments or fair comparison protocols) but represent gaps in the current submission. The work makes meaningful advances in an important application domain and establishes a new benchmark, but lacks the polish and completeness expected of top-tier work. The extensive appendices demonstrate significant effort, but key practical considerations (scalability, real bidder data) warrant fuller treatment in future work."

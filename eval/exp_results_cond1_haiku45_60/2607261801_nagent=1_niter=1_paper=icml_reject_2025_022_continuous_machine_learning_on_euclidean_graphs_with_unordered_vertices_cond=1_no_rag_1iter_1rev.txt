      "summary_comment": "This paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: defining complete and continuous invariants for Euclidean graphs with unordered vertices. The theoretical contribution is solid—introducing the Nested Centered Distribution (NCD) with formal guarantees of completeness, Lipschitz continuity, invertibility, and polynomial-time computability (Theorem 4.6). However, the work suffers from critical weaknesses that prevent acceptance at a top-tier ML conference: (1) **Soundness issues**: The completeness proof for the core result (Theorem 4.6b) is relegated to appendices without intuitive justification in the main text. The strength modification σ to ensure Lipschitz continuity lacks clear motivation and necessity arguments. (2) **Significance and evaluation gaps**: The complete invariant NCD(G;3) has prohibitive complexity (>10¹¹ operations for realistic inputs), contradicting claims of practical efficiency. Experiments only validate fast invariants (SRD, SPD) on element prediction, not the novel complete invariant on graph isomorphism. No comparative benchmarks against GNNs or existing descriptors are provided. (3) **Presentation**: The paper is dense and difficult to follow, with notation overload and key proofs deferred to appendices. The connection to machine learning is unclear—no new learning algorithms are proposed. (4) **Experimental rigor**: Tables 4-5 report only minimal observed distances without statistical validation, ablations, or comparisons. Claims like 'all chemically different molecules are rigidly different' are asserted without proof. The triangle axiom is proven theoretically but never empirically validated. For a selective ML conference, the work reads more as a theoretical mathematics contribution with insufficient experimental validation and practical applicability. The hierarchical invariant structure is pragmatic, but the paper fails to demonstrate that the complete invariant provides value beyond the faster approximations already shown to work well in practice."

      "summary_comment": "This paper presents ElasticMM, a well-motivated and thoughtfully designed system for efficient multimodal LLM serving. The core insight—that MLLM serving requires both modality-level and stage-level adaptation through elastic scheduling—is valuable and addresses real limitations in existing tightly-coupled systems. The two-tier architecture with modality-aware load balancing and elastic partition scheduling is novel, and the multimodal-specific optimizations (unified prefix caching, non-blocking encoding) are practical contributions. Experimental results demonstrate substantial improvements (up to 4.2× TTFT reduction, 3.2-4.5× throughput improvement) over vLLM and DistServe baselines on representative MLLM architectures using real-world datasets. The evaluation includes appropriate ablations isolating individual contributions. However, the work has notable limitations: (1) evaluation restricted to a single-node 8×A800 setup without multi-node experiments, significantly limiting applicability to production-scale deployments; (2) lack of formal theoretical analysis of the scheduling algorithms or convergence guarantees; (3) no experimental comparison with concurrent MLLM-specific systems (ModServe, EPD) despite mentioning them in related work; (4) limited exploration of generalization across diverse MLLM architectures, GPU types, and emerging modalities (video, audio) mentioned in the abstract. The gain-cost models appear reasonable but lack principled guidelines for hyperparameter setting (e.g., penalty factor w). Despite these limitations, the system design is sound, the technical approach is novel within its scope, and the practical improvements are significant. The paper makes a solid contribution to an important and timely problem, though the single-node evaluation and narrow baseline comparisons prevent it from being a strong accept. The work would benefit from multi-node evaluation and additional baseline comparisons before deployment in production systems, but the core contributions are valuable for the MLLM serving research community."

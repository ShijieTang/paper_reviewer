However, the paper suffers from significant weaknesses that prevent acceptance at a top-tier venue. First, the technical novelty is limited—the core contribution is an incremental modification of UGC (replacing bin-widths with sorted list merging) combined with existing techniques. Second, the theoretical justification is insufficient: theorems about projection proximity and load balance do not establish that adaptive coarsening preserves graph quality across resolutions. Third, the experimental evaluation has critical gaps: (a) Section 4.1 only measures coarsening time without validating end-to-end benefits or comparing adaptive vs. recomputed quality; (b) classification accuracy results are mixed and often inferior to baselines; (c) claimed support for streaming and expanding graphs is not validated. While the paper tackles important problems and demonstrates competitive performance on heterogeneous graphs, the limited novelty, insufficient theoretical grounding, and incomplete experimental validation of key claims make it unsuitable for publication at a top-tier venue."

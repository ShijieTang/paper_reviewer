      "summary_comment": "This paper makes solid theoretical and methodological contributions by extending consistency distillation to unnormalized sampling without data access. CDDS cleverly uses intermediate ODE trajectories and SCDS introduces an innovative step-size-conditioned control function that enables self-distillation during training. The framework is theoretically grounded with Theorem 1 providing convergence guarantees. The work addresses an important computational problem in sampling and demonstrates that consistency-based acceleration is feasible in the unnormalized setting. However, the paper has a significant gap between theory and empirical validation. Single-step sampling—the central claimed contribution—produces results that are often substantially worse than multi-step baselines (50x worse Sinkhorn distance on some tasks, catastrophic normalization constant errors). The paper acknowledges this as a quality trade-off but presents it within a narrative of 'high-fidelity samples,' which is misleading. SCDS consistently underperforms CDDS without clear characterization of when to use it. Key ablations are missing, experimental scope is limited to low dimensions, and baseline comparisons are narrow. The true contribution appears to be demonstrating that few-step sampling (8-64 steps) can match or exceed baseline performance with training-time consistency losses, which is valuable but less novel than single-step sampling would be. The flexibility to trade computation for quality is practically useful, but this amounts to a competitive alternative to existing methods rather than a substantial advance. The paper merits publication as a solid contribution with novel techniques, but the empirical results do not match the optimistic framing of the abstract and introduction. The work would be stronger with honest acknowledgment of single-step limitations and clearer characterization of when the proposed methods are preferable to existing alternatives."

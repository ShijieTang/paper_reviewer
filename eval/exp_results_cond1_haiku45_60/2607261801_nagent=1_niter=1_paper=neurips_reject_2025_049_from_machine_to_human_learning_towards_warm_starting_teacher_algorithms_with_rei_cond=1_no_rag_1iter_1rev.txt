While the motivation is sound and human experiments are conducted, the work has significant limitations. Novelty is limited—the core idea is relatively straightforward application of existing curriculum learning and domain randomization techniques. PERM-H is a minor modification (changing one parameter) of PERM, and SimMAC applies standard Wasserstein distance metrics conventionally. Significance is undermined by: (1) evaluation limited to two simple game-based domains without demonstrated generalization, (2) Section 6.3 reveals substantial RL-human misalignment (e.g., humans vastly more efficient in Jumper; only moderate correlation r=0.49 in task similarity), raising questions about whether RL agents are truly suitable proxies, (3) no quantitative evidence that total effort (RL training + validation + human data) is actually reduced compared to baselines, and (4) small sample sizes without power analysis. The paper makes a reasonable pragmatic contribution but falls short of the novelty and significance bar for a top-tier ML conference. The work would be strengthened by: broader domain evaluation, deeper analysis of when RL bootstrapping fails, quantification of effort reduction, and better characterization of the RL-human gap and its implications."

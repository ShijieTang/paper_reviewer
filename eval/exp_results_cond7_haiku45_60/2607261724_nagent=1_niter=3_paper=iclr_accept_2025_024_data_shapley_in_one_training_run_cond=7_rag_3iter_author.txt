The core technical contributions are sound: (1) Taylor expansion-based closed-form Shapley formulas enabling efficient computation; (2) 'ghost' techniques achieving 30x+ speedup over naive implementation while maintaining accuracy (RMSE~0.0003); (3) conceptual reframing from algorithm-level to model-specific attribution. The paper is clearly written with interesting case studies on real pretraining data (Pile), yielding insights into data curation and copyright implications. The rebuttal demonstrates mature understanding of limitations: the authors correctly distinguish between addressable gaps (approximation validation across phases, small-scale baseline comparisons, checkpoint selection guidance) and fundamental constraints (validation data timing, SGD-only support, model size demonstrations). However, significant gaps remain in the submitted paper: (1) Approximation quality validated at only one checkpoint (iteration 3500), not systematically across training phases or learning rates; (2) Foundation model applicability claimed but not demonstrated—experiments stop at 410M parameters with no computational analysis of billion-parameter scaling; (3) No empirical analysis of checkpoint-based workaround for validation data timing; (4) No preliminary work toward Adam support despite being critical for modern ML. These gaps are not fatal—the core contributions remain valuable—but they represent incomplete validation of key claims. For acceptance at a top venue, the paper demonstrates sufficient novelty, soundness, and practical impact. The limitations are appropriately acknowledged and do not undermine core contributions, though they do define current applicability scope. The work will likely inspire follow-up research extending to larger scales, modern optimizers, and post-hoc applicability scenarios."

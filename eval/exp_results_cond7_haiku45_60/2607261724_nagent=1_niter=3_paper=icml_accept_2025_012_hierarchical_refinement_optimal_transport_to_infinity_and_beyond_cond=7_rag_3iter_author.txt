      "summary_comment": "This paper makes a solid contribution to scalable optimal transport by proposing Hierarchical Refinement (HiRef), an algorithm achieving linear space and log-linear time complexity for computing bijective Monge maps on large-scale datasets. The core theoretical innovation—Proposition 3.1 establishing that optimal low-rank OT factors co-cluster points with their Monge images—is novel and mathematically rigorous. Lemma B.5 rigorously establishes that this property holds automatically for rank-2 factorizations, directly covering the practical binary schedules used in experiments. The algorithm elegantly recurses through low-rank OT subproblems to build increasingly fine partitions culminating in a bijective Monge map. Experimental validation across synthetic, biological (transcriptomics, spatial), and image domains is comprehensive and convincing: Tables 1-2 show superior primal costs versus Sinkhorn/ProgOT on problems those methods cannot solve; Figure S3 demonstrates monotonic cost improvement; MERFISH results (Table S7) show HiRef cosine similarities >0.79 versus <0.34 for competing methods. The method scales to 1.28M points on ImageNet, a significant practical achievement. The acknowledged gap between theoretical optimality (assuming optimal LROT) and practical performance (using heuristic LROT) does not undermine the work—the rebuttal clarifies that Lemma B.5 provides direct guarantees for binary schedules actually used, and empirical results demonstrate practical effectiveness. Main limitations—equal dataset size requirement, non-convex LROT subproblem, and schedule ablations—are minor and do not materially affect the contribution. The work is technically sound, well-presented, and makes both theoretical and practical contributions enabling new applications of OT to modern large-scale datasets, addressing a widely recognized bottleneck in the field.

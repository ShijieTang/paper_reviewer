## 242 **3.1 A Practical Algorithm Based On Pac-Bayes Rl**

243 Our algorithm, PAC-Bayes-Certified Soft Actor–Critic (*PB-SAC*), operationalizes the PAC-Bayes 244 value-error bound of Theorem 3.2 within a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) training loop. Building upon 245 the periodic update cycle described earlier, PB-SAC maintains a posterior distribution over policy 246 parameters and injects sampled knowledge to guide exploration. While it shares the "distribution247 over-policies" principle with EPICG and EPICG-SAC [29], our approach differs in three fundamental 248 ways: (i) It focuses on **single-task optimization** rather than task streams, though the framework 249 naturally extends to Lifelong RL scenarios. (ii) It explicitly optimizes **the exact value-error bound** 250 from Theorem 3.2 during training and **monitors the bound value** at each update cycle, providing 251 continuous performance guarantees. (iii) It applies the posterior distribution **directly to actor** 252 **parameters** θ rather than relying on critic ensembles or Bellman-error surrogates as in PBAC, 253 resulting in a more streamlined and computationally efficient implementation. Additionally, PBAC 254 [14] trains critics via a Catoni-type Bellman-error bound but never reports the bound value; it is used 255 only as a loss. EPICG/EPICG-SAC of [29] regularizes the KL between posterior and running prior 256 yet does not compute or log the PAC-Bayes bound either. 257 **Posterior, prior, and sampling.** Let θ denote the flattened parameters of the actor network. The posterior ρ is a diagonal Gaussian, θ ∼ N (υ, diag(σ 2 258 )), where both the mean υ and standard 259 deviation σ are learnable, gradient-tracked variables. The *prior* µ is simply a copy of the posterior at 260 initialization, or after a PAC-Bayes update cycle; this choice maintains meaningful guarantees by 261 preventing KL(ρ∥µ) from exploding while ensuring that the core SAC algorithm can continue learn262 ing. Additionally, without this resetting mechanism, the KL(ρ∥µ) regularization would permanently 263 penalize deviations from the initial actor parameters, significantly hindering the learning process. 264 **PAC-Bayes update cycle.** Our algorithm performs periodic PAC-Bayes updates using completely 265 new data batches to maintain theoretical guarantees. At each update point, we freeze the current policy 266 and collect a fresh batch of T trajectories, which are used exclusively for the current PAC-Bayes 267 analysis. Using this batch, we fit a posterior distribution ρ by optimizing Lρ = Eθ∼ρ[−Q(*s, π*θ(s))]+
268 βKL(ρ∥µ), balancing critic values against divergence from the prior. This rapid tightening stems primarily 322 from the simulator's quick mixing dynamics, which benefit our PAC-Bayes bound, additional data 323 rapidly sharpens the certificate. For **HalfCheetah**, the bound improves more gradually, reflecting 324 the environment's longer effective horizon where states remain correlated over extended periods.

325 Finally, **Humanoid** represents the most challenging scenario with its numerous degrees of freedom 326 and complex dynamics. Although guarantees become comparatively looser in this environment, they 327 consistently remain informative, never deteriorating to the trivial bound of zero. 328 **Guided exploration without sacrificing reward.** Turning to Fig. 2, PB-SAC matches the baselines 329 on Sepsis, keeps pace on HalfCheetah, and modestly outperforms SAC on Humanoid. The results suggest that safety 332 certificates and competitive return need not be at odds. Across tasks we observe a clear narrative: 333 the faster the environment mixes, the faster the PAC-Bayes certificate closes the gap to the critic.

We therefore recommend including 336 mixing-time diagnostics in future evaluations of safe RL methods. 337 In summary, PB-SAC converts a theoretically principled bound into a live learning signal: it produces 338 meaningful confidence certificates early, preserves or improves return, and exhibits behavior that 339 aligns with the qualitative dependence on Markov mixing predicted by our analysis.

## 340 **5 Conclusion And Limitations**

341 In this work, we introduced PB-SAC, a PAC-Bayesian actor–critic algorithm that advances the 342 intersection of reinforcement learning and Bayesian guarantees. If adapted to the EPICG/EPICG-SAC 343 framework [29], we believe PB-SAC holds the potential to address the well-known *plasticity–stability* 344 dilemma, a prominent research challenge in lifelong and continual reinforcement learning. This 345 extension would allow the agent to balance the retention of useful prior knowledge (*stability*) with the 346 acquisition of new information (*plasticity*), using PAC-Bayesian guarantees as a principled mechanism 347 for managing uncertainty. Empirically, PB-SAC matches or surpasses SAC on both clinical and 348 continuous-control benchmarks, while yielding confidence bounds that tighten predictably with 349 the environment's mixing time. This marks a step forward toward certified reinforcement learning 350 algorithms suitable for real-world deployment, particularly in high-stakes domains where reliable 351 performance guarantees are essential. 352 **Limitations.** Our current framework uses a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence penalty between 353 prior and posterior over policy parameters. Although KL is analytically convenient, it does not respect 354 the intrinsic geometry of the parameter space and can exhibit unstable behavior when distributions 355 diverge significantly [36]. In high-dimensional settings, computing KL gradients is computationally 356 intensive and may force the posterior to collapse onto the prior—resulting in overly constrained 357 updates that hinder meaningful learning progress [37]. 358 An appealing alternative is to employ a Wasserstein distance within the PAC-Bayes bound. Recent 359 work has developed high-probability PAC-Bayesian inequalities based on Wasserstein metrics, which 360 naturally capture distributional geometry and avoid degenerate update regimes even when supports are 361 disjoint [38, 39, 36]. Moreover, entropic (Sinkhorn) smoothing enables scalable stochastic variational 362 inference under Wasserstein regularization, making posterior updates tractable in high dimensions 363 [40]. Incorporating a Wasserstein-based PAC-Bayes bound and a corresponding Sinkhorn-SVI
364 scheme is a key direction for future research, with the potential to yield tighter certificates and more 365 robust policy learning.

$$\begin{array}{l}{{\delta_{t+1}(C)=r(C)+\gamma\operatorname*{max}_{a}\mathbb{E}[V(s^{\prime})|s=C,a]-V(C)=+1+0\cdot V(C)-0=+1}}\\ {{\delta_{t+1}(D)=r(D)+\gamma\operatorname*{max}_{a}\mathbb{E}[V(s^{\prime})|s=D,a]-V(D)=-1+0\cdot V(D)-0=-1}}\end{array}$$

591 This simple example demonstrates that knowing the current Bellman error δt = 0 is insufficient to 592 determine the distribution of the next Bellman error δt+1, which can be either +1 or −1 depending 593 on the state that produced the current error.

594 For a sequence to be Markovian, the conditional distribution of future states must depend only on 595 the current state, not on the sequence of events that preceded it. In this case, the distribution of δt+1 596 depends on which state (A or B) produced δt = 0, not just on the value of δt itself. 597 Therefore, the sequence of Bellman errors {δt} cannot be modeled as a Markov chain in general, 598 invalidating a key assumption in the theoretical analysis of Tasdighi et al. [14].

621 The PAC-Bayesian framework introduces several additional hyperparameters: 622 - PAC-Bayes update frequency of 20,000 environment steps 623 - KL regularization coefficient β = 1.0 624 - Posterior knowledge injection coefficient λ = 0.01 625 - Failure probability δ = 0.05 (95% confidence level) 626 - Initial maximum reward estimate Rmax = 1.0 627 - 10,000 rollout trajectories for bound computation 628 - 75 steps per rollout trajectory 629 The infrequent PAC-Bayes updates (every 20,000 steps) are a critical design choice that balances 630 computational efficiency with the need to maintain accurate performance guarantees. This allows 631 the base SAC algorithm to make rapid progress between bound computations while ensuring the 632 posterior distribution properly tracks policy improvements. 633 We deliberately set the posterior knowledge injection coefficient λ to a small value (0.01) to ensure 634 that the standard SAC optimization process dominates learning, while the PAC-Bayesian posterior 635 provides a stabilizing influence and theoretical guarantees. This proved more effective than larger 636 values, which tended to slow convergence by disrupting the actor's learning dynamics. 637 For bound computation, we found that 10,000 rollout trajectories of 75 steps each provides sufficiently 638 accurate estimates of the mixing time and expected returns for our environments. These rollouts are 639 performed with deterministic policies to accurately reflect the posterior's expected performance. 640 The PAC-Bayes updates maintain a posterior distribution over policy parameters, optimize it using 641 a combination of critic values and KL regularization, and periodically inject information from this 642 posterior into the actor network. This process allows us to derive high-probability bounds on policy 643 performance without significantly hampering the learning capabilities of the base SAC algorithm.

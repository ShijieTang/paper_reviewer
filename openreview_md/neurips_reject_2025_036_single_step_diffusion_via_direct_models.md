## Abstract 12 **1 Introduction**

13 Diffusion models and flow matching methods have recently achieved remarkable success across 14 a wide range of applications, including image synthesis [5], audio generation [7], and 3D shape 15 modeling [10]. Despite their effectiveness, a significant limitation of these approaches lies in their 16 reliance on iterative sampling or inference procedures, which are computationally expensive and can 17 limit real-time deployment. 18 To address this bottleneck, some recent works have explored distillation techniques to compress 19 multi-step diffusion or flow matching models into efficient single-step samplers. Notable examples 20 include [12, 16], which require first training a high-quality teacher model and then performing a 21 costly distillation step. Moreover, such distillation sometimes involves constructing large synthetic 22 datasets, increasing the complexity and resource demands of the overall pipeline. 23 In contrast, this work proposes a novel *single-run training* approach that directly learns a *one-step* 24 diffusion-like generative model without relying on teacher models or distillation. Our method offers a 25 more practical and efficient solution for fast sampling, avoiding the overhead inherent in multi-stage 26 training pipelines and results in high-quality samples (see Figure 1). 27 To address these challenges, we propose a new class of *Direct Models*, a residual-based formulation 28 that enables both single-step sampling and single-run training. The key idea is to directly model the 29 full flow map through a time-indexed residual field, allowing us to query any intermediate latent 30 xt without requiring numerical integration. This direct access to latents motivates the name of our 31 approach. 32 At the core of our method lies a simple recursive structure: the residual displacement at time t + δt is 33 expressed as a combination of the residual at time t and the local velocity at that point. This recursive 34 formulation not only serves as a training objective but also acts as a structural prior, encouraging 35 consistency across time steps while remaining efficient and fully self-supervised. 36 Our main contributions are as follows:

## 190 **6 Related Work**

191 We briefly review existing approaches that enable single-step diffusion-based generation, which can 192 be broadly categorized into distillation-based methods and single-phase training methods. 193 **Distillation Methods** In recent years, various techniques have been developed to distill generative 194 models, particularly diffusion models, into more efficient one-step sampling frameworks. These 195 methods typically follow a two-stage pipeline: first, a diffusion model is pretrained; second, a separate 196 model is trained to approximate the behavior of the full diffusion process using fewer inference steps. 197 Methods such as knowledge distillation [12] and rectified flows [9] generate synthetic training 198 pairs by fully simulating the reverse-time denoising ODE. Due to the high computational cost of 199 full simulation, more efficient alternatives have been proposed that use bootstrapping strategies to 200 partially initialize the ODE trajectory [4, 21]. Additionally, several works have explored alternatives 201 to the standard L2 loss, including adversarial training objectives [17] and distribution-matching 202 approaches [23, 22]. Progressive distillation techniques [16, 1, 13] further decompose the distillation 203 process into multiple stages with increasing time step sizes, thereby reducing the need for long 204 bootstrap paths.

205 In contrast to these methods, we propose an end-to-end training approach that directly learns a 206 one-step generative model. This eliminates the need for separate pretraining and distillation phases, 207 making our method simpler than both full simulation-based techniques and multi-stage progressive 208 distillation. 209 **Single-phase Training Methods** Few methods have been proposed for single-phase training that 210 enable single-step generation. Consistency Models [20], a pioneering approach in this area, represent 211 a class of generative models that directly map partially noised data points to their final, fully denoised 212 outputs in a single step. While these models have been effectively used in distillation purposes, 213 they have also been explored the end-to-end training scenario. Shortcut models [3] propose a novel 214 generative modeling framework that conditions on both the current noise level and desired step size, 215 enabling efficient and flexible sampling under different inference budgets. 216 **Difference from consistency models [20]** While we share the general concept of consistency 217 with [20], Direct Models differs significantly in its formulation. Conceptually, Direct Models can 218 be seen as the opposite approach: instead of mapping intermediate latents xt directly to the fully 219 denoised image x1, our method maps initial Gaussian noise x0 to all intermediate latent states xt, as 220 shown in Figure 3. 221 **Difference from shortcut models [3]** Shortcut models are arguably the most similar approach to 222 Direct Models. However, there are key differences: 1) Conceptually, Shortcut models learn direct 223 mappings between all pairs of latent states, including both the initial and final ones. In contrast, 224 Direct Models focuses on learning a direct mapping only between the initial Gaussian noise and 225 all intermediate latent states. 2) More importantly, our approach is grounded on a more principled 226 mathematical formulation. Specifically, we derive our method using a Taylor expansion of the flow, 227 as shown in Equation (13), which links the flow at neighboring timesteps to the velocity field.

## 228 **7 Limitations And Future Work**

229 Our method presents some limitations. First, Direct Models requires training two separate networks, 230 which limits efficiency. Second, the current formulation is restricted to single-step inference. A 231 promising direction for future research is to extend our framework to enable training a single unified 232 model while potentially allowing flexibility in the number of inference steps during sampling.

## 233 **8 Conclusion**

234 We introduced Direct Models, a new type of diffusion-based generative model that enables both 235 single-step sampling and single-run training. By learning a time-indexed residual field to directly 236 approximate the full generative flow, our method achieves fast and high-quality generation while 237 significantly simplifying the training process. This makes Direct Models a practical and efficient 238 alternative to existing diffusion-based techniques.
